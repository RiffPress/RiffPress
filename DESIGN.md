# Design

## Product Character

Riff is a music player that sits inside someone else's site. The player is always dark and always the same; the page around it belongs to the theme. Administration uses WordPress conventions without a separate branded shell: fields in the Media Library, such as Artwork (the attachment's own featured image), Video version and Protected.

## Visual System

### The player

YouTube Music's current layout and measurements are the authority, except the wide bar's arrangement, which follows Tidal, and Now Playing, which is a view at a time, each taking the whole screen above the bar and opened from its button at the bar's right, as Spotify's views are (the lyrics), the album from a press on the bar itself (the cover where Now Playing is upright and the turntable where it is wide), with Up next a panel beside the page: the 72px bar on a wide screen, the 2025 Now Playing on a phone, Roboto, and `#212121` surfaces on `#030303`; the icons are WordPress's own in place of Material's. Riff's own mark is one accent taken from the cover playing (`--riff-cover-accent`), or the site's own where a cover has none, in place of red, the dark-mode mirror of the site's charcoal on paper. The bar's seek line is plain, as YouTube Music's is: a thin track, what has loaded in a quieter shade, the played part in the accent, and a knob only while it is pointed at, in focus or dragged. It moves on the compositor. Now Playing's own seek line is plain white. The volume, on a wide bar, is a short slider drawn the same way, beside its mute button, in a slot of its own, so it never opens over the controls beside it.

### The Listen block

The library page follows the theme. On a block theme it takes the theme's background, text and link colours and its fonts from global styles, and mixes the quieter shades from them. On a classic theme it uses WordPress.org's design system: its palette, Inter for text, EB Garamond for headings, and 2px corners.

### Rows in the post

Tracks are rows inside the post's own content and take the theme's colours through `currentcolor`. They add nothing the theme has to style.

## Layout

Phone: the bar along the bottom with its progress line on the top edge; Now Playing full-screen with the Song/Video switch at the top, square art, title and artist, a thick thumbless seek bar with the quality as small print between its times, the controls, action pills, and a chip for each view, a panel's (Up next, About) opening it over everything as a sheet. The Listen app on a phone, as the apps lay theirs out: one slim row at the top with the site's name and who is listening, Albums, Artists, Tracks and Posts as chips under it on Home and on those lists, and Home, Search and Library as tabs along the bottom with the bar sitting on them; Search opens a view of its own, the field and the lists as tiles. Wide screen: the bar is laid out as Tidal's, the track on the left, shuffle, previous, play, next and repeat in the middle with the seek line and both times under them, where they are easy to reach, and the quality pill, the views' buttons and the volume on the right; a press on the bar, the track at the left or anywhere but a control, opens Now Playing. Now Playing fills the space above the bar with the view open, a panel beside it when one is open: Up next, or About, the track's cover, title, artist and album and the credits its tags carry, as Spotify's Now Playing column has them. Wide and short on a touch screen (an unfolded iPhone Duo, a phone on its side): the screen is the turntable, the deck filling its height on the left with Start · Stop, 33 and 45 and a pitch fader on its plinth, and the sleeve in a rack above the controls in a strip down the right; an album opens as a gatefold, the cover on the left page and the tracks on the right. Short and narrow (a folded iPhone Duo): Home, Search and Library down the right edge, as iOS sets its own bars there. Folded part way, nothing lands in the crease: like a book, the deck and the controls each keep to a page; stood like a tent, Now Playing is upright, so the cover stands above the controls.

The art is a record in its sleeve. Where Now Playing is wider than it is tall (a computer, an unfolded phone, a phone on its side), the record slides out once the album plays and the sleeve is set down beside it, each as large as the stage allows. On a phone held upright the cover stands alone at full size, and play and pause act at once. The record is cut as a 12-inch LP: a label a third of its width printed in the cover's colours, smooth dead wax, a band of grooves for each track as wide as the track is long, lighter where the track is loud when its loudness has been measured, and a smooth lead-in rim. Where the files' tags number the discs of a set, each disc is a record of its own. A side played to its end leaves the stylus in the run-out's locked groove for two turns, clicking once a turn, before the arm goes back. It turns clockwise at 33⅓, scaled by the playback speed, and comes up to speed and stops as a direct-drive platter does, its speed changing in a straight line. The tonearm, set up by Baerwald's alignment with its anti-skate dial beside it and the target light by the platter, tracks the album from the outside in, so each angle is one moment of it, and swings to a new track. Whatever a hand moves (the record, the sleeve, the arm) moves on a hand's path; the record drops its last few millimetres onto the mat, and the cue lever lowers the arm at its damping's even speed. A new album puts the record back in its sleeve and the next one comes in. With the experimental Riff Meters plugin, a VU meter and a spectrum under the deck read the music itself, in the player's ink on the dark: the meter's needle moves as the standard's does, reaching a steady tone's reading in 300 ms, and the spectrum is a bar for each third of an octave, averaged over a second, so it shows the music's shape, and where a lossy file's top stops, rather than each beat. Neither is drawn where the music can't be measured (an iPhone, a file from another origin that doesn't share), for a listener who asks for less motion, or with the turntable off. The Albums view can also stand its cards in a rack, spines out, each spine in its cover's colour.

On a computer, in a browser and installed alike: Spotify's frame. A 64px black strip with back and forward at its left end, clear of the window's controls where it is the title bar, Home and the search pill in the middle as one group, and who is listening at its right; under it the library and the view as `#121212` panels, 8px apart with 8px corners, on black; the bar black below them. Where a panel beside them leaves the page too little room for an album's rows, as on a window around 1024px wide, the library folds to its rail until the panel closes. The keyboard reaches the strip left to right before the library. The pill asks in Riff's own words. Its app icon is the wordmark in amber on a charcoal tile, on Apple's icon grid (`site/brand/`).

## Interaction

Tap a row to play, tap the bar for Now Playing, swipe the bar to skip, drag Now Playing down to close. Motion is transform and opacity only. Reduced motion removes the transitions and stills the playing row's bars.

## Accessibility

Native `<audio>` and `<video>` underneath, a native range for seeking, labelled buttons whose labels change with state, a dialog that makes the page inert and returns focus, visible focus rings, and 44px targets on a phone.
